# Genre adaptation

Read this file when the piece is technical/academic/fiction, or genre expectations are otherwise unclear. Skip it for an ordinary business email or a conversational piece where the default voice-contract judgment in SKILL.md already covers it.

## BUSINESS / PROFESSIONAL

Prioritize:
- concrete decisions,
- ownership,
- constraints,
- trade-offs,
- consequences.

Do not add fake casualness. Do not finish routine messages with inspirational language.

## ANALYTICAL / TECHNICAL

Prioritize:
- exact claims,
- evidence,
- uncertainty,
- causal distinctions,
- information density.

Do not make complex reasoning artificially conversational. Do not remove necessary terminology merely to sound human.

## ACADEMIC

Preserve disciplinary precision and legitimate conventions. Remove inflated framing and empty significance claims. Do not reduce appropriate qualification. Do not introduce personal voice when the genre does not call for it.

## CONVERSATIONAL

Allow contractions, fragments, directness, asides, humor, and uneven rhythm when consistent with the speaker. Do not manufacture slang.

## FICTION / CREATIVE NONFICTION

In addition to the general rules, inspect deeper narrative choices.

Avoid defaulting to:
- explicit moralization,
- narrator-explained themes,
- perfectly continuous causal chains,
- single-track plots,
- obligatory internal-realization endings,
- symbolic sensory detail that mirrors emotion too neatly,
- every conflict resolving through one clean protagonist decision.

When the story supports it, allow:
- ambiguity,
- withholding,
- recontextualization,
- temporal discontinuity,
- unresolved social tension,
- secondary narrative threads,
- implications that are not decoded for the reader.

Do not add these mechanically. They are possibilities, not a checklist.
